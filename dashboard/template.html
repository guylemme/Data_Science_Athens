How do monitoring stations compare?

Compare long-term pollutant concentrations and count hourly observations above the project's PM2.5 reference level. Each station contributes its available observations.

Reading the chart: higher bars indicate higher concentrations or more exceedance observations. The 25 µg/m³ hourly reference is a project indicator; it is not presented as a regulatory compliance test.

How this indicator is calculated

For each station, mean PM2.5 and NO₂ are the sums of valid hourly concentrations divided by their valid observation counts. Maximum PM2.5 is the largest valid concentration. Exceedance hours count records where PM2.5 > 25 µg/m³. Missing concentrations are excluded from means. These station comparisons span the full dataset period.

How the association indicators are calculated

Wind magnitude = √(U² + V²). Hourly variables are averaged per station and day. PM2.5, humidity, temperature and wind are classified as Low (≤ Q1), Medium (Q1 to Q3) or High (> Q3). Days missing any analysis variable are removed. Apriori uses support ≥ 5%, itemsets up to length 4; rules use confidence ≥ 10%. Only rules with exactly high PM2.5 as the consequent are shown. Support = P(conditions and high PM2.5); confidence = P(high PM2.5 | conditions); lift = confidence / P(high PM2.5).

Can we anticipate daily PM2.5?

A historical 30-day holdout evaluates the model before it is refitted to forecast the 30 days following the dataset. These forecasts concern 2023, not current air quality.

Holdout error · µg/m³

Lower MAE and RMSE are better. The seasonal-naive reference repeats the last seven training days. One holdout period does not establish performance in every season.

How forecasting and evaluation work

Hourly PM2.5 is averaged per station-day, then equally across available stations each day. The final 30 days are withheld. Gaps are filled only within the training history. Holt-Winters uses an additive damped trend and additive weekly seasonality (7 days). Predictions are clipped at zero. MAE = mean |observed − predicted|; RMSE = √mean (observed − predicted)², evaluated only on observed test days. The final model is refitted on the full historical series to forecast a further 30 days. No prediction intervals are included.

Where are the geographical station groups?

DBSCAN groups nearby monitoring stations using their coordinates. Pollution is compared after grouping; concentrations do not determine cluster membership.

How spatial clusters are calculated

Each station must have fixed valid coordinates. Latitude and longitude are converted to radians; DBSCAN uses the haversine distance, a 2 km neighborhood and at least 3 stations per dense neighborhood (including the station itself). Label −1 means spatial noise, not an erroneous sensor. For each station, daily mean PM2.5 values are averaged; cluster concentration is the unweighted mean of member-station means. Noise stations are excluded from cluster averages. Distances determine these groups, not pollution similarity.

Scope and limitations

The source combines monitoring locations including CAMS and named stations. Station density and station types can affect representativeness. A monitoring-network average is not a population exposure estimate.

Extreme concentrations are retained unless negative or infinite. They may affect means and errors and should be investigated before stronger conclusions.

Quartile categories are descriptive. Weather associations can reflect seasonal and spatial differences; no causal claim is made.

Dashboard controls filter already computed indicators. They do not retrain the forecast or change DBSCAN parameters.

No external enrichment dataset is used. Instructor approval of the dataset is not recorded in the repository.
